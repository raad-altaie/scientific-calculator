# Scientific Calculator

A scientific calculator for your browser: light, fast and it works offline. It runs as a Chrome extension (open it with the toolbar icon or `Alt+S`) and as a website version in `web/`.

## What it does

**Calculating**
- Real scientific-calculator order of operations (PEMDAS): `1+2×3 = 7`, and `1×(2+3) = 5`, then `= 25`.
- The display shows the whole equation while you type it; after `=` it shows the result with the finished equation on a small line above.
- Parentheses nested up to 29 levels deep; no need to close them before `=`.
- `DEL` (or Backspace) takes back the last key; `C` clears the number; the cursor keys or a click fix a number or operator in the middle of an equation.
- Select part of an equation, then type or paste to replace it, or `DEL` to remove it.
- History of past calculations: insert or copy the equation or the result, or delete one. Export, beside Clear, copies the whole history as text (`1 + 2 = 3`, a line each) or saves it as `calculator-history-YYYY-MM-DD.csv`, for a spreadsheet, or `.txt`.
- Memory keys (`mc`, `m+`, `m-`, `mr`) and `Ans` for the last answer.
- Everything is remembered when you close it, including the equation, memory and settings.

**Functions**
- Powers and roots: `x²`, `x³`, `yˣ`, `√`, `ˣ√y`, `1/x`, `abs`, `x!`.
- Trigonometry: `sin`, `cos`, `tan`, their inverses, and the hyperbolic `sinh`, `cosh`, `tanh` and inverses.
- Logarithms and exponentials: `log`, `ln`, `log₂`, log to any base, `eˣ`, `2ˣ`, `10ˣ`; constants `π` and `e`.
- Combinatorics and number theory: `nCr`, `nPr`, `gcd`, `lcm`, `mod`, `%`, `Rand`, `EE` for scientific entry.
- Angle units: Deg, Rad and Grad. `°′″` shows results as degrees, minutes and seconds, and `12°30′15″` can be pasted.
- Fraction mode (`a/b`) draws each division as a fraction and shows results like 0.75 as 3/4.
- Number format: Fix, Sci or Eng notation, Auto or 0 to 9 decimal places, and thousands separators with the decimal mark of your country: `1,234.5`, `1 234.5`, `1.234,5` or `1 234,5`.
- Function keys go before the number (`√ 9`); x², x³, x!, 1/x and % go after it or before it (`5 x²`, `x² 5`). A two-number key pressed first, as nCr, takes the last result.
- Ten letters, `A` to `J`, each keep a value, as on a TI or a Casio. `Alt+A` (Option+A on a Mac) is STO→ A: type `5 + 3`, press `Alt+A`, and 8 is kept in A (or `>` then `Shift+A`), the history saying `5 + 3 → A`. `Shift+A` to `Shift+J` put a letter in the equation, where it stays a letter, `2A + B`, and is worked out with what it holds; the history notes what each letter held. The **Variables** tab of the formulas lists them to type over, store in or insert, and a right-click on the display or on a value of a panel or the history has **Store in Var**; on the display, **Insert Var** puts one in with the mouse. They work in pasted text (`2A + B`), the graph (`A x^2`, a capital never a slider), the solver, and every box that takes a number (a formula's, the converter's, the graph's and the numbers'), where a box takes only digits and `A` to `J` and resting on it shows what they hold; they start at 0. EE shows as `ᴇ` (`5ᴇ3`), so `2E` is 2 × E.

**Panels** (each can be turned on or off in the options)
- **Vars, formulas and constants** (`fx`): it opens on **Variables**, the letters A to J and what each holds, the first time, then on the tab you used last. Search well-known formulas of algebra, geometry, trigonometry, physics, electricity, chemistry, statistics, money and everyday life, fill in the letters and the equation goes into the display; a box takes a stored letter too, `A` or `2B`. Constants of math and science (CODATA 2022) go in with a click.
- **Converter**: length, area, volume, mass, temperature, speed, time, data, pressure, energy, power, angle and fuel economy; the value is shown in every other unit of its kind. Its **Dates** tab gives the time between two dates in years, months and days first (`29 February 2000` to `14 October 2026` is 26 years, 7 months, 15 days), then the whole of it in years, months, weeks, days, weekdays and hours, and moves a date on by years, months and days, back below 0, with its weekday, day of the year and ISO week; a month from 31 January is the last day of February; and gives the date and time of a Unix timestamp (`1700000000` is 14 November 2023, 22:13:20 UTC; of 13 digits, milliseconds) in UTC, the time here and ISO 8601, or the timestamp of a date and time. Dates are written as the browser's region writes them, in the boxes and the results (`14/10/2026` in the UK and France, `10/14/2026` in the US), typed so or as `14 Oct 2026` or `2026-10-14`, or picked from the calculator's own calendar, which a click in the box opens, its weeks starting on the region's first day; an empty date is today.
- **Graph**: type up to seven functions of x, a box for each that the + adds, as you would paste them (`x^2 - 3`, `2sin(x)`, `1/x`) and they are drawn on x and y axes, each in its color. Any other small letter gets a slider, a capital A to J being the value stored in it: drag `a` and `b` of `a x^2 + b` and the curve follows, or ▶ plays a slider back and forth. Roots, highs and lows, inflection points, where a curve meets the y axis and where two cross are marked with dots. Drag to move, scroll or pinch to zoom; point at a curve or a dot for its x and y, a round number near it going to it, 2 and not 2.04, and click it for a popup that inserts its x or y into the calculation (it goes three and a half seconds after the mouse leaves it, and at once on a zoom, leaving the point and its tangent; a click on the point brings it back), or on a curve its slope there, with the tangent dashed; the arrow keys then trace it, ← → along the curve, stopping at roots and crossings, ↑ ↓ to the next curve; the frame under home sets the view by typing where x and y go from and to; off the curves, a click gives the place itself. For the area under a curve, press the button with a shaded curve under the zoom buttons and drag along the curve: the area to the x axis is shaded and its popup inserts it, below the axis counted down, between two curves if started between them, its ends going to a root or crossing near them, else to a round number, or typed in its popup; the graph still as you drag but sliding on where the area is held at its left or right side; the button stays pressed for another area, until it is pressed again, a double click on the graph, Esc, or a button of the view. Click a curve's y′ to draw its slope, the derivative, dashed, or type `d/dx(x^2)` for a slope as a curve of its own; equations of x and y are drawn too, `x = 3`, `x^2 + y^2 = 25`, pointed at, clicked and traced as curves are, where they meet the axes and cross a curve marked; inequalities are shaded where they are true, `y < x^2`, `x^2 + y^2 <= 9`, `1 < x < 3`, with the area inside one, or inside an equation, and where two equations cross marked; polar and parametric curves, `r = 2sin(3θ)`, `(cos t, sin t)`, `r = θ {0, 6π}`; sequences drawn as dots at each whole n, recursive `a(n) = a(n−1) × 1.05 {a(1) = 100}`, Fibonacci `a(n) = a(n−1) + a(n−2) {a(1) = 1, a(2) = 1}` or explicit `a(n) = 2n + 1`, n from 1 to 50 unless braces say, `{1, 100}`, up to 10000 terms, pointed at and traced by n, listed in the table; slope fields, `dy/dx = x - y {y(0) = 1}`, a short line at each place at its slope, with the solution through each start, a click adding one; points, `(1, 2), (2, 4.1)`, typed or in a table, with a best fit and its r²: a line, a parabola, an exponential, a power or a logarithm; functions named, `f(x) = x^2`, used in other boxes, `f(x) + 1`, `f'(x)`; curves kept to where inequalities hold, `x^2 {0 < x < 3}`, or in pieces, `{x < 0: -x, x^2}`; click its dot to hide or show it; save the graph as a picture with its functions, or right-click it to copy one. Pop out makes the graph big: all of the window on the website, a window of its own beside the calculator in the extension; on the website pop in makes it small again. The **Table** tab lists x and each curve's y, from a start by a step that follow the view until you set them; click a value to insert it. The **Examples** tab has some sixty graphs to try, by group as the constants are, and last the fun ones that move as they are drawn, a pendulum, an atom, the Earth and Moon going round the Sun, a golden spiral; click one and it is drawn in place of your curves. Functions of an angle follow Deg, Rad or Grad.
- **Solve** (`x=`): every root of an equation of x (`x^3 = 2x + 1`), in exact form as well (`√2`, `(1 + √5)/2`, `π/6`), where an inequality holds (`x^2 - 4 > 0` is `x < -2 or x > 2`), the turning points of a function, the real and complex roots of a quadratic, cubic, quartic or any polynomial up to the power 30 (`(x - 1)(x + 2)^2`, or its coefficients), two or three linear equations, and where two curves meet (`x^2 + y^2 = 25` and `x + y = 7`); integrals from a to b (an end may be `inf`) in exact form as well (`√π`) and the average of the function, the value and slopes of a function at x and its tangent line, sums and products to `inf` as well (`1/n^2` is `π²/6`), limits from below and above (`sin(x)/x` at 0, `(1+1/x)^x` at `inf`), Taylor series about a point, the area between two curves, arc length, and differential equations `y′ = f(x, y)`; on the **Finance** tab, money over time as a BA II Plus works it out (N, I/Y, PV, PMT and FV, the empty one found, money received above 0 and paid below), NPV, IRR and payback of cash flows, a loan's payment schedule by year or by payment with Copy table for a spreadsheet, savings growth with amounts added and a table of each year, APR ↔ APY, a bond's price or yield with its duration and accrued interest, depreciation by the straight line or the declining balance, break-even, inflation, and percentage change, markup and margin, money shown to the cent; normal probabilities, density and inverse, Student's t, χ² and F with p-values and critical values, binomial, Poisson, geometric, hypergeometric, exponential and uniform chances, confidence intervals of a mean and a proportion, and random whole numbers. Worked out as you type; click a result to insert it.
- **Statistics**: type or paste a list (a spreadsheet column works too) for count, sum, sum of squares, mean, standard deviation (σ and s), variance s², minimum, the quartiles (as a TI-84 finds them), median, maximum, range, interquartile range and mode, with a histogram and a box plot. The **Pairs** tab takes x and y on each line, or two spreadsheet columns, for their means, standard deviations, sums, Σxy, covariance and the line of best fit `y = a + bx` with r and r², drawn through the pairs. Each tab keeps its own numbers. It finds what is between the numbers by itself; choose new lines, tabs, spaces, commas, semicolons, pipes or colons instead, and numbers written `1,234.5`, `1 234,5` or `$1,200` are read whole.
- **Numbers** (`[i]`): matrices up to 10 × 10 (boxes and results smaller to fit the card; a note says when a badly conditioned one, as the Hilbert matrix, cannot be trusted) with their determinant, rank, inverse, transpose and n-th power, the sum and products of two, their eigenvalues and eigenvectors, `A x = b` with every answer where there are many and least squares where there are none, the rows reduced and the null space, LU and QR, a whole matrix pasted into a box from a spreadsheet or as `[1 2; 3 4]`, Copy on a matrix of results, and an Example on each card; vectors, their dot and cross products, lengths, angle and projection; complex numbers, `(2+3i)(1-i)`, `sqrt(-4)`, `2∠30`, with their parts, length, angle, conjugate, `r e^(iθ)` and n-th roots, drawn on the plane, the roots of polynomials of complex coefficients, and the AC impedance of R, L and C in series or parallel (`10µ`, `4.7k`) with its phase, the reactances and resonance; whole numbers in 8 to 128 bits, `0xFF AND 0b1010`, `0x81 ROL 1`, shown in decimal, hexadecimal, octal and binary, with their bytes big-endian, little-endian and swapped, their bits to turn over with a click; any base from 2 to 36, `0.1` as `0.0(0011)` in base 2; text as its code points, UTF-8 and UTF-16, and back; text in Base32, Base58, Base64 and Base85 (Ascii85) and back; the MD5, SHA-1, SHA-256, SHA-512 and CRC-32 of a text, and a hash pasted checked against them; new UUIDs of version 4 and 7, five at a time, each to copy, and a UUID read for its version, variant, the time it was made, and it in hexadecimal and Base64; the IPv4 subnet of `192.168.1.10/24` or an address and mask, its network, broadcast, mask, first and last host and hosts, and the IPv6 subnet of `2001:db8::1/48`, the address in full and short, its range, addresses, /64 networks, kind and reverse DNS name; a number as an IEEE 754 double and float, `0.1 = 0x3FB999999999999A`, to its last digit; the prime factors and divisors of a whole number of up to 20 digits, the GCD and LCM of a list, modular arithmetic and the Chinese remainder theorem, exact big numbers (`100!`, `2^200`, `50 nCr 25`, `fib(300)`), primes (next, previous, π(n), the n-th, those between two numbers), fractions and repeating decimals (`0.1(6) = 1/6`) with continued fractions, and a number rounded to d places or significant figures. Click a result, or a number of a matrix, to insert it.

**Keyboard, copy and paste**
- Rest the pointer on a result of Solve, Numbers, the dates or the statistics to see it in full, cut short in its tile as a long one is, with what it is where it has a tip, and Insert and Copy.
- Full keyboard support for every button, on any layout: on Arabic, Hebrew, Russian or Greek a key gives what the key of its place gives on a US keyboard, in the boxes of the panels too (`٥` types 5, Shift+`ش` types A, a pasted `٣٫٥` is 3.5); a `h` hold key gives the second function (`h`, `s` = `sinh`). Shift+A to J are the letters, so cos⁻¹ is Shift+V, EE Shift+W, floor Shift+Y and gcd Shift+Q.
- Ctrl+Z (⌘Z) undoes the last change to the equation, and Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes it.
- Ctrl+C / Ctrl+V and a right-click menu per area; the display's has **Reset all**, which clears the equation, the memory, the letters, the modes and what is typed in every panel, the history staying. You can paste a whole equation such as `2 + 3 × sin(30)` or `sqrt(16) + 2^3`; `1,234.5`, `1.234,5` and `1 234,5` are all read as 1234.5, and a currency is left out: `$1,200` or `5 USD` is the number alone.

**The extension**
- Opens from the toolbar icon or `Alt+S`.
- **Detached Mode**: the calculator in its own window that stays open and resizes with it.
- **Open mode**: the toolbar icon and Alt+S open the popup (Fixed, the default) or the detached window. With Fixed, Detached Mode is still in the menu of the toolbar icon and on the calculator button on every page.
- **Calculator on every page** (optional): a small button on the side of every page that opens Detached Mode. It asks for website access only when turned on, and reads nothing from the pages.
- No permission is needed to read your browser data.

**For schools and organizations**: an administrator can turn features off for everyone they manage, for a class during a test for example. A feature turned off goes from the calculator at once, its option on the options page is locked off with "Turned off by your administrator", and an open window of it closes. Each one is on unless its policy is `false`:

| Policy | Turns off |
| --- | --- |
| `formulas` | Formulas and constants |
| `converter` | The converter, of units and of dates |
| `graph` | The graph, with its own window |
| `solve` | Solve: equations, calculus and probability |
| `statistics` | The statistics |
| `numbers` | Numbers: matrices, vectors, complex numbers, bases, text codes, prime factors, modular arithmetic, primes, exact numbers, fractions and rounding |
| `detachedMode` | Detached Mode, and with it the calculator on every page, which opens it |
| `pageButton` | The calculator on every page |

In the Google Admin Console: **Devices › Chrome › Apps & extensions › Users & browsers**, choose the organizational unit, add the extension by its ID (shown on `chrome://extensions`) and paste into **Policy for extensions**:

```json
{
    "graph": { "Value": false },
    "formulas": { "Value": false }
}
```

Elsewhere it is the extension's policy of Chrome, as [`managed_schema.json`](managed_schema.json) describes: on Windows the DWORD values (0 for off) under `HKEY_LOCAL_MACHINE\Software\Policies\Google\Chrome\3rdparty\extensions\<ID>\policy`, on macOS a profile for the domain `com.google.Chrome.extensions.<ID>`. `chrome://policy` shows what the browser has.

**Precision**: about 15 significant digits (IEEE 754 doubles), shown to 13 so that `0.1 + 0.2` is `0.3`.

## Website version

`web/` is a standalone copy of the calculator for the web: the calculator on the left, the options on the right. On a screen narrower than 1100px (phones and iPads) the options stay hidden until you click the `?` on the calculator. Host the folder as-is on any static host, or run `python3 -m http.server` inside it. It does not need the extension.

The calculator is part of the page itself, in the extension's `popup.html` and in `web/index.html`: there is no frame around it. After changing the calculator in the extension, run `scripts/sync-web.sh` to copy it into `web/`: its styles and scripts, and its markup, which goes from `popup.html` into `web/index.html` between the two `calculator` comments. What the web changes (base font size, where the calculator sits) is kept in `web/css/site.css`, so the copies never need hand edits.
