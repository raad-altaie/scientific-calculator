Scientific Calculator v 2.2

Formulas and constants

A button at the top left of the display with well-known formulas of geometry, physics, money and more, and constants of math and science, all searchable. Fill in a formula's letters to put it into the display, where you can still change it.

Converter

A button at the top left of the display that converts units of length, area, volume, mass, temperature, speed, time, data, pressure, energy, power, angles and fuel economy. The result shown is the value to start from; click a converted value to put it into the display.

Graph

A button at the top left of the display that draws up to seven functions of x, such as x^2 – 3 or 2sin(x), on x and y axes, with their roots, highs, lows and crossings marked. Drag to move it, scroll or pinch to zoom, and point at a curve or a dot for its values; the Table tab lists them.

Solve

A button at the top left of the display that solves an equation or an inequality for x, a polynomial or two or three linear equations, finds integrals, slopes, sums, products, limits and differential equations, and the chances of the normal, binomial and Poisson distributions.

Statistics

A button at the top left of the display: type or paste a list of numbers for its count, sum, mean, standard deviation, min, median and max.

Numbers

A button at the top left of the display for matrices: their determinant, inverse, sums, products and eigenvalues, and A x = b, complex numbers, such as (2 + 3i)(1 – i), whole numbers in bits with their hexadecimal, octal and binary, and numbers as doubles and floats, and whole numbers' prime factors, GCD and LCM, and rounding.

Features

  • History: click the clock at the top left of the display to see your past calculations. Insert an equation or a result into the display again, copy it, or delete it. It is kept when you close the calculator.
  • DEL button (or Backspace) to take back the last key you pressed, one key at a time.
  • Cursor: click into the equation, or use the arrow keys, to fix a number or an operator in the middle of it. Back at the end, the calculator goes on as before.
  • Formulas and constants: some hundred formulas to fill in and sixty constants of math and science. See the "Formulas and Constants" section below.
  • Converter: units of length, area, volume, mass, temperature and more. See the "Converter" section below.
  • Graph: functions, equations, inequalities and points drawn, with their roots, highs, lows and crossings. See the "Graph" section below.
  • Solve: every root of an equation, inequalities, polynomials, linear systems, integrals, slopes, sums, limits, differential equations and probability. See the "Solve" section below.
  • Statistics: count, sum, mean, standard deviation, min, median and max of a list of numbers. See the "Statistics" section below.
  • Numbers: matrices and their eigenvalues, complex numbers, bases and bits, IEEE 754, prime factors and rounding. See the "Numbers" section below.
  • mod, gcd and lcm on the 2nd layer of %, nCr and nPr.
  • floor and ceil on the 2nd layer of abs and x!, side by side, and round on that of Rand: a number to the whole number below or above it, and to the nearest, half away from 0. They can be pasted too, with trunc, which goes toward 0: floor(7.5) = 7.
  • 10x, e and logy (the logarithm to any base: 8 logy 2 = 3) on the 2nd layer of log, π and yx.
  • Degrees, minutes and seconds: the °′″ key shows results as 36°52′11.63″, and DMS in the display shows it is on.   • Real sci-calc-style calculations, so 1+2*3=7 (... not 9 like with others),

  • No need to close parentheses if equation sign follows anyhow.

Formulas and Constants

Click fx at the top left of the display. It has two tabs, Formulas and Constants, each with a search: type a few words, area circle or interest, and only what has them all is listed. Enter in the search opens the first one found.

  • Some hundred formulas, by group: algebra, geometry, trigonometry, motion and forces, matter and energy, waves and light, electricity, chemistry, statistics, money and everyday ones. The quadratic formula, the volume of a sphere, the law of cosines, Newton's second law, Ohm's law, pH, compound interest, a loan payment, a price after discount, body mass index or a bill split among friends.
  • Rest the pointer on a formula to see what it gives, what each of its letters stands for and an example: a = 1, b = −3, c = −4 gives 4.
  • Click a formula for a box for each of its letters. A result in the display is already in the first box, to start from. Enter in a box goes on to the next empty one.
  • Insert, or Enter once all are filled, puts the formula into the display with its letters filled in, as an equation you can still change before =. Into an equation you are typing it goes in brackets, as one part of it.
  • Values with no answer stay in the boxes, and the formula says why in place of Error: "No real roots: b² − 4ac is below 0."
  • A formula with angles takes them in the unit in use, Deg, Rad or Grad, and says so under its boxes.
  • Some sixty constants, by group: math, universal, atoms and particles, heat and chemistry, and Earth and space. π, e and the golden ratio, the speed of light, the Planck, Avogadro and Boltzmann constants, the mass of the Earth, the Moon, Mars and the Sun, a light-year and more, those of physics from CODATA 2022. Each shows its symbol, its value and its unit; click one to put it into the equation as a number, in full and not only the 12 digits shown.

Converter

Click the arrows at the top left of the display. Pick what to convert and the unit your value is in, and the list shows it in all the other units of its kind at once, worked out as you type.

  • Thirteen kinds: length, area, volume, mass, temperature, speed, time, data, pressure, energy, power, angles and fuel economy. From millimeters to nautical miles, teaspoons to US and UK gallons, kilobytes to tebibytes, calories to kilowatt-hours, horsepower, knots, and miles per gallon to liters per 100 km.
  • The result in the display is the value to start from.
  • Click a converted value, or press Enter for the first one, to put it into the equation. Right-click one to copy it, alone or with its unit.
  • Exact where a unit is defined exactly: an inch is 2.54 cm, a pound 0.45359237 kg. Very large and very small values are shown as powers of 10.
  • An angle can be typed and shown in degrees, minutes and seconds, 36°52′11″, as well as in degrees, radians, gradians and turns.
  • The kind, the units and the value stay for the next time.

Graph

  • Click the curve at the top left of the display and type a function of x, as you would paste it: x^2 – 3, 2sin(x), 1/x or sqrt(x). The + at the top adds a box for another curve, up to seven, each in its own color, and the × in a box removes its curve.
  • Any other letter than x gets a slider: in a x^2 + b, drag a and b to see the curve change, or type their values and the ends of the slider; ▶ plays a slider, its value going from one end to the other and back with the curve, until you pause it.
  • Dots mark where a curve is 0 (its roots), its highs and lows, where it bends the other way (its inflection points), where it meets the y axis, and where two curves cross.
  • Drag to move the graph, scroll, pinch or use + and – to zoom, and the crosshair to go back to the start.
  • Point at a curve or a dot for its x and y, a round number near it going to it, 2 and not 2.04; click it for a popup where a click on its x or y puts it into your calculation (the popup goes three and a half seconds after the mouse is off it, and at once as you zoom, the point and its tangent staying; click the point to see it again), and on a curve the slope there as well, its tangent dashed across the graph. The arrow keys then trace it: ← and → move it along its curve, stopping at a root or a crossing, Shift ten times as far, and ↑ and ↓ take it to the curve above or below.
  • The button with a frame, under home, sets the view by typing where x and y go from and to. Off the curves, a click gives the x and y of that place.
  • For the area under a curve, press the button with a shaded curve, under the zoom buttons, and drag along the curve from one x to another: the area to the x axis is shaded, below the axis counted down; started between two curves, it is the area between them; an end near a root or a crossing goes to it, else to a round number, and its popup inserts the area; type in its From x and To x for ends of your own. The button stays pressed for another area, and the graph does not move as you drag, until you press it again, Esc, or zoom, home or the view.
  • Click a curve's y′ to draw its slope, the derivative, dashed in its color, or type d/dx before a function for its slope as a curve of its own: d/dx(x^2) is 2x.
  • An equation of x and y is drawn too, where its two sides are equal: x = 3, x^2 + y^2 = 25, x = y^2, its letters with sliders as well; point at it or click it as at a curve, for its x, y and slope, where it meets the axes and crosses a curve marked, and trace it with ← and →.
  • An inequality is shaded where it is true: y < x^2, x^2 + y^2 <= 9, x > 2, its line dashed for < and >, as its edge is not in it; two at once, 1 < x < 3, where both are.
  • A polar curve, r = 2sin(3θ), and a parametric one, (cos t, sin t), go once round, or as far as {from, to} after them says, r = θ {0, 6π}; click one for its θ or t, and trace it.
  • Type points, (1, 2), (2, 4.1), (3, 5.9), and their fit draws the line nearest them, with its r²; click fit again for a parabola, an exponential (a · bˣ), a power and a logarithm, or the table button beside it to type them in rows of x and y.
  • Name a function, f(x) = x^2, and other boxes can use it: f(x) + 1, f(2x), or its slope f'(x) and f''(x).
  • Inequalities in braces keep a curve to where they hold, x^2 {0 < x < 3}, sin(x) {y > 0}; with a colon a function goes in pieces, {x < 0: -x, x^2}, the first that holds.
  • Where two equations cross is marked, and the area button finds the area inside an equation or an inequality too, as of x^2 + y^2 = 25.
  • Click a curve's colored dot to hide it and again to show it. The arrow down at the lower right saves the graph as a picture with its functions; right-click the graph to copy the picture instead. Pop out, the arrow out of a box beside Clear, makes the graph big: all of the window on the website, where pop in or Esc makes it small again, and in a window of its own beside the calculator in the extension.
  • The Table tab lists x and the y of each curve, from a start by a step; left empty, they follow the view of the graph. Click a value to put it into your calculation.
  • The Examples tab has graphs to try, by group as the constants are: functions, sliders, equations, inequalities, polar and parametric curves, pieces, functions named and points. Click one, or search and press Enter, and it is drawn in place of your curves.
  • Functions of an angle follow Deg, Rad or Grad.

Solve

Click x= at the top left of the display. Each tool is a card: open it, type in its boxes, and it is worked out as you type. Click a result to put it into your calculation.

  • Solve finds every root of an equation of x, x^3 = 2x + 1 or sin(x) = 0.5 (From and To say where to look), where an inequality holds, x^2 – 4 > 0 as x < –2 or x > 2 and in intervals, the real and complex roots of a quadratic, a cubic or a quartic, and x, y and z of two or three linear equations.
  • Calculus has the integral of a function from a to b, an end of inf going on without end, its value and slopes at x, sums and products for n from a to b, the limit of a function as x comes to a, from below and above, sin(x)/x at 0 or (1 + 1/x)^x at inf, and y at x of a differential equation, y′ = x + y with y(0) = 1.
  • Probability has the normal distribution's chances, density and inverse, binomial and Poisson chances, and random whole numbers.
  • A box of a number takes a calculation too: pi/4, sqrt(2). What you typed stays for the next time.

Statistics

Click the bars at the top left of the display and type or paste a list of numbers, a column from a spreadsheet as well, for its count, sum, mean, standard deviation (σ and s), min, median and max. Click a result to use it in your calculation. The list finds what is between its numbers by itself; choose new lines, tabs, spaces, commas, semicolons, pipes or colons at the top of the panel instead, and a number written 1,234.5, 1 234,5 or $1,200 is read whole.

Numbers

Click the brackets with an i at the top left of the display. It has cards as the solver has, on four tabs. Click a result, or a number of a matrix, to put it into your calculation.

  • Matrix has a matrix of up to 4 × 4, its determinant, rank, inverse, transpose and square, and the sum and products of two; its eigenvalues, real or complex, its characteristic polynomial and its eigenvectors; and A x = b, linear equations of up to four unknowns.
  • Complex works out a complex number, (2 + 3i)(1 – i), sqrt(-4) or 2∠30 in polar form, for its real and imaginary parts, length, angle and conjugate, and the n-th roots of one.
  • Base works out whole numbers as a computer keeps them, in 8, 16, 32 or 64 bits: decimal, 0xFF, 0b1010 or 0o17, with + – × ÷, mod, AND, OR, XOR, NOT, shifts and ROL and ROR, shown in decimal, hexadecimal, octal and binary. Bits shows each bit of a number, to turn over with a click, and how many are 1; IEEE 754 shows a number as a double and a float, 0.1 = 0x3FB999999999999A, what it is to its last digit, and the number of the bits of one.
  • Integer has the prime factors of a whole number of up to 20 digits, 360 = 2³ × 3² × 5, whether it is prime, and its divisors; the GCD and LCM of two numbers or more; and a number rounded to d places, half away from 0, down, up and toward 0, and to d significant figures.

Using the Calculator

Click on numbers and functions in Calculator just as you would with a standard calculator.
The display shows the equation as you enter it, so you always see the operation to be carried out. Click the equation sign and the display shows the result, with the finished equation on the small line above it. The opened parenthesis button keeps a ring around it until all parentheses expressions are closed.

If an equation gets too long for the display, its font gets smaller first. After that, press the line, hold it still for a moment, then drag it left or right to see the rest; dots (…) mark the side where more is hidden. A drag without the hold selects a part of it. On a touch screen a drag slides it, and a hold selects.
If a calculation is not possible (for example 1 ÷ 0), the display shows Error and the line above shows the equation that caused it.

This calculator uses an algebraic method of entry that allows you to enter mathematical sequences in the same order that they are algebraically stated (PEMDAS / AOS™).
PEMDAS: "Parentheses, Exponents, Multiplication or Division, and Addition or Subtraction". Multiplication and division have the same ranking. Addition and subtraction as well.

Keyboard Support

There is full keyboard support for each button on the calculator.

  • The numbers and + – × ÷ , . = keys can also be input on number block.
  • See below the keyboard shortcuts of each calculator button in [brackets].
  • Use the [Backspace] key or the DEL button to take back the last key you pressed: a digit, an operator, a bracket or a function. A finished result stays.
  • Hold key: [h, s] means: press h or H and you'll see a little 'hold' in the right bottom corner of the display.
    This makes the followed key function different: [s] = sin, but [h, s] = sinh, etc.
  • Copy/Paste from/to display with Ctrl+C and Ctrl+V. Ctrl+C copies the result or, while you are still entering an equation, the equation as text.
    Ctrl+V pastes a number (1,234.5, 1.234,5 and 1 234,5 are all read as 1234.5; 1.234 alone is read with the decimal mark the options set, and $1,200 or 5 USD as the number alone) or a whole equation, such as 2 + 3 × sin(30) or sqrt(16) + 2^3. If the pasted text contains an equation sign, it is calculated right away.
    Double-click a number in the equation to select it whole, or drag over a part of it: what you type or paste then goes in its place, and DEL takes it out.
    Right-click the display for a menu with Cut, Copy and Paste as well. Copy takes the part of the display you selected, or else the result. The first time you use Paste there, your browser may ask you to allow reading what you copied; Ctrl+V works without that. The history, the formulas, the converter and the statistics have the menu too: Cut, Copy, Paste and Select all in a box you type in, Copy and Insert on a value.
  • All [ENTER] keys represent the '=' button.
  • Click Rad, Deg or Grad at the bottom left of the display to go on to the next unit, as the key does.
  • [←] and [→] move the cursor in the equation. [Home] and [End] go to its start and its end.
  • History: click the clock at the top left of the display to see the calculations you finished with '='. Point at an equation or a result to show its two buttons on the left: insert enters the equation again, so you can change it or finish it with '=', or puts the result in as a number in the equation you are entering; copy copies it. A calculation with sin, cos or tan is marked with the unit it was done in, Deg, Rad or Grad, and is worked out in that unit again. A calculation done in fraction mode keeps its fractions, and one done with decimals keeps its decimals; inserting it switches fraction mode to match.

[← →]

About Precision

This calculator operates with a precision of about 15 significant digits (only). This is due to how JavaScript handles floating point numbers.
This isn't actually a JavaScript feature but a feature of the way the IEEE 754 standard defines how floating point numbers are stored in memory. JavaScript requires every number to be one of these 64-bit values, and any language that uses them, such as C, Java or Python, suffers from this 'feature' too.

Results are shown with 13 significant digits, so these tiny rounding errors stay out of sight: 0.1 + 0.2 shows 0.3, not 0.30000000000000004. Angles in degrees give exact values where they should: sin 180 is 0 and cos 60 is 0.5.

The problem is that JavaScript uses 64 bits of data, which can hold 18446744073709551616 different bit patterns, to store every number: 1 bit for the sign, 11 bits for the exponent and 52 bits for the digits. That covers values from about –1.7976931348623157e+308 to 1.7976931348623157e+308, with the smallest normal number at 2.2250738585072014e–308 and even smaller ones down to 5e–324.
A smaller precision value with some of the bits as an exponent is how it fits that range (this can also be done in 16 bits with a smaller range and less precision). The result is that it can approximate any value in the range but not exactly represent them all, because in real number terms there are an infinite number of values between any 2 given values.
As part of your calculation clearly the internal representation of the value is going outside the available precision and you are ending up with an approximation to the value instead of an exact value.
