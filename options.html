Scientific Calculator

Formulas and constants

A button at the top left of the display with well-known formulas of geometry, physics, money and more, and constants of math and science, all searchable. Fill in a formula's letters to put it into the display, where you can still change it.

Converter

A button at the top left of the display that converts units of length, area, volume, mass, temperature, speed, time, data, pressure, energy, power, angles and fuel economy. The result shown is the value to start from; click a converted value to put it into the display. Its Dates tab counts the days between two dates and moves a date on by years, months and days.

Graph

A button at the top left of the display that draws up to seven functions of x, such as x^2 – 3 or 2sin(x), on x and y axes, with their roots, highs, lows and crossings marked. Drag to move it, scroll or pinch to zoom, and point at a curve or a dot for its values; the Table tab lists them.

Solve

A button at the top left of the display that solves an equation or an inequality for x, a polynomial, two or three linear equations or two curves, finds turning points, integrals, slopes, sums and products to ∞, limits, Taylor series, areas between curves, arc lengths and differential equations, the chances of the normal, t, χ², F, binomial, Poisson and other distributions, confidence intervals, and money over time, NPV and IRR, payment schedules and APR and APY.

Statistics

A button at the top left of the display: type or paste a list of numbers for its count, sum, mean, standard deviation, quartiles, median and more, with a histogram and a box plot; or pairs of numbers for their line of best fit and correlation.

Numbers

A button at the top left of the display for matrices: their determinant, inverse, powers, sums, products, eigenvalues, rows reduced, LU and QR, and A x = b, up to 10 × 10, and vectors; complex numbers, such as (2 + 3i)(1 – i), and the roots of polynomials of them; whole numbers in bits with their hexadecimal, octal and binary, numbers in any base, text as its codes, and numbers as doubles and floats; and whole numbers' prime factors, GCD and LCM, modular arithmetic, primes, numbers to every digit, fractions and their decimals, and rounding.

Formulas and Constants

Click fx at the top left of the display. It has two tabs, Formulas and Constants, each with a search: type a few words, area circle or interest, and only what has them all is listed. Enter in the search opens the first one found.

  • Some hundred formulas, by group: algebra, geometry, trigonometry, motion and forces, matter and energy, waves and light, electricity, chemistry, statistics, money and everyday ones. The quadratic formula, the volume of a sphere, the law of cosines, Newton's second law, Ohm's law, pH, compound interest, a loan payment, a price after discount, body mass index or a bill split among friends.
  • Rest the pointer on a formula to see what it gives, what each of its letters stands for and an example: a = 1, b = −3, c = −4 gives 4.
  • Click a formula for a box for each of its letters. A result in the display is already in the first box, to start from. Enter in a box goes on to the next empty one.
  • Insert, or Enter once all are filled, puts the formula into the display with its letters filled in, as an equation you can still change before =. Into an equation you are typing it goes in brackets, as one part of it.
  • Values with no answer stay in the boxes, and the formula says why in place of Error: "No real roots: b² − 4ac is below 0."
  • A formula with angles takes them in the unit in use, Deg, Rad or Grad, and says so under its boxes.
  • Some sixty constants, by group: math, universal, atoms and particles, heat and chemistry, and Earth and space. π, e and the golden ratio, the speed of light, the Planck, Avogadro and Boltzmann constants, the mass of the Earth, the Moon, Mars and the Sun, a light-year and more, those of physics from CODATA 2022. Each shows its symbol, its value and its unit; click one to put it into the equation as a number, in full and not only the 12 digits shown.

Graph

  • Click the curve at the top left of the display and type a function of x, as you would paste it: x^2 – 3, 2sin(x), 1/x or sqrt(x). The + at the top adds a box for another curve, up to seven, each in its own color, and the × in a box removes its curve.
  • Any other letter than x gets a slider: in a x^2 + b, drag a and b to see the curve change, or type their values and the ends of the slider; ▶ plays a slider, its value going from one end to the other and back with the curve, until you pause it.
  • Dots mark where a curve is 0 (its roots), its highs and lows, where it bends the other way (its inflection points), where it meets the y axis, and where two curves cross.
  • Drag to move the graph, scroll, pinch or use + and – to zoom, and the crosshair to go back to the start.
  • Point at a curve or a dot for its x and y, a round number near it going to it, 2 and not 2.04; click it for a popup where a click on its x or y puts it into your calculation (the popup goes three and a half seconds after the mouse is off it, and at once as you zoom, the point and its tangent staying; click the point to see it again), and on a curve the slope there as well, its tangent dashed across the graph. The arrow keys then trace it: ← and → move it along its curve, stopping at a root or a crossing, Shift ten times as far, and ↑ and ↓ take it to the curve above or below.
  • The button with a frame, under home, sets the view by typing where x and y go from and to. Off the curves, a click gives the x and y of that place.
  • For the area under a curve, press the button with a shaded curve, under the zoom buttons, and drag along the curve from one x to another: the area to the x axis is shaded, below the axis counted down; started between two curves, it is the area between them; an end near a root or a crossing goes to it, else to a round number, and its popup inserts the area; type in its From x and To x for ends of your own. The graph does not move as you drag, but held at its left or right side it slides on that way, the area growing with it. The button stays pressed for another area, until you press it again, double-click the graph, Esc, or zoom, home or the view.
  • Click a curve's y′ to draw its slope, the derivative, dashed in its color, or type d/dx before a function for its slope as a curve of its own: d/dx(x^2) is 2x.
  • An equation of x and y is drawn too, where its two sides are equal: x = 3, x^2 + y^2 = 25, x = y^2, its letters with sliders as well; point at it or click it as at a curve, for its x, y and slope, where it meets the axes and crosses a curve marked, and trace it with ← and →.
  • An inequality is shaded where it is true: y < x^2, x^2 + y^2 <= 9, x > 2, its line dashed for < and >, as its edge is not in it; two at once, 1 < x < 3, where both are.
  • A polar curve, r = 2sin(3θ), and a parametric one, (cos t, sin t), go once round, or as far as {from, to} after them says, r = θ {0, 6π}; click one for its θ or t, and trace it.
  • A sequence is drawn as a dot at each whole n: a(n) = a(n – 1) × 1.05 {a(1) = 100}, each term of the ones before it, its first terms in braces after it as a polar curve has its range; a(n) = a(n – 1) + a(n – 2) {a(1) = 1, a(2) = 1}; or a(n) = 2n + 1, of n alone. n goes from the first term given, or 1, for 50 terms, or as two numbers in the braces say, {1, 100}, up to 10000 terms. Any lowercase letter but x, e and i names one, and the same letter alone is still a slider. Point at a dot or click it for n and a(n), and ← and → go from term to term; a term too large to draw stops it, and one of a term before the first given is said in the note.
  • Type points, (1, 2), (2, 4.1), (3, 5.9), and their fit draws the line nearest them, with its r²; click fit again for a parabola, an exponential (a · bˣ), a power and a logarithm, or the table button beside it to type them in rows of x and y.
  • Name a function, f(x) = x^2, and other boxes can use it: f(x) + 1, f(2x), or its slope f'(x) and f''(x).
  • Inequalities in braces keep a curve to where they hold, x^2 {0 < x < 3}, sin(x) {y > 0}; with a colon a function goes in pieces, {x < 0: -x, x^2}, the first that holds.
  • Where two equations cross is marked, and the area button finds the area inside an equation or an inequality too, as of x^2 + y^2 = 25.
  • Click a curve's colored dot to hide it and again to show it. The arrow down at the lower right saves the graph as a picture with its functions; right-click the graph to copy the picture instead. Pop out, the arrow out of a box beside Clear, makes the graph big: all of the window on the website, where pop in or Esc makes it small again, and in a window of its own beside the calculator in the extension.
  • The Table tab lists x and the y of each curve, and of a sequence its terms, n by 1 where there are only sequences, from a start by a step; left empty, they follow the view of the graph. Click a value to put it into your calculation.
  • The Examples tab has graphs to try, by group as the constants are: fun pictures that move, functions, sliders, equations, inequalities, polar and parametric curves, sequences, pieces, functions named and points. Click one, or search and press Enter, and it is drawn in place of your curves.
  • Functions of an angle follow Deg, Rad or Grad.

Solve

Click x= at the top left of the display. Each tool is a card: open it, type in its boxes, and it is worked out as you type. Click a result to put it into your calculation, or rest the pointer on it to see it in full, with Insert and Copy.

  • Solve finds every root of an equation of x, x^3 = 2x + 1 or sin(x) = 0.5 (From and To say where to look), where an inequality holds, x^2 – 4 > 0 as x < –2 or x > 2 and in intervals, the turning points of a function, its highs and lows, the real and complex roots of a quadratic, a cubic, a quartic or a polynomial of any power up to 30, typed as (x – 1)(x + 2)^2 or as its coefficients, x, y and z of two or three linear equations, and x and y where two curves meet, x^2 + y^2 = 25 and x + y = 7. A root that has an exact form shows it as well: √2, (1 + √5)/2, π/6, ln 2.
  • Calculus has the integral of a function from a to b, an end of inf going on without end, its exact form, √π or 1/3, and the function's average there; its value and slopes at x and the tangent line there; sums and products for n from a to b, b inf as well, 1/n^2 from 1 on as π²/6; the limit of a function as x comes to a, from below and above, sin(x)/x at 0 or (1 + 1/x)^x at inf; its Taylor series about a, sin x as x – x³/6 + x⁵/120 – …; the area between two curves and the length of one from a to b; and y at x of a differential equation, y′ = x + y with y(0) = 1.
  • Probability has the normal distribution's chances, density and inverse; Student's t, χ² and F, their chances, p-values and the value for a chance; binomial, Poisson, geometric and hypergeometric chances; exponential and uniform ones; confidence intervals of a mean and of a proportion; and random whole numbers.
  • Finance works money out as a BA II Plus or an HP 12C does. Time value of money takes N, I/Y (a percentage a year), PV, PMT and FV, with the payments a year and each at the end of its period or at its start: leave one of the five empty and it is worked out, with all the money paid and the interest. Money received is above 0 and money paid below 0, so a loan of 200000 is PV = 200000 with PMT below 0; where the signs cannot balance, the note says so. NPV and IRR takes a rate and cash flows, one to a line or apart as the statistics list is, the first at the start: their net present value, internal rate of return, the one nearest 10% where the flows change sign more than once, and payback period. Payment schedule gives a loan's payment, its interest and a table of each year or, up to 600 rows, each payment, of the interest, the principal and what is left; Copy table copies it for a spreadsheet. Interest rates turns an APR into its APY for m periods a year, ∞ as well, and back. Money is shown to the cent; a click or Insert puts it in with every digit.
  • A box of a number takes a calculation too: pi/4, sqrt(2). What you typed stays for the next time.

Numbers

Click the brackets with an i at the top left of the display. It has cards as the solver has, on four tabs. Click a result, or a number of a matrix, to put it into your calculation; rest the pointer on a result to see it in full, with Insert and Copy.

  • Matrix has a matrix of up to 10 × 10, its determinant, rank, inverse, transpose and power n, and the sum and products of two; its eigenvalues, real or complex, its characteristic polynomial and its eigenvectors; A x = b, linear equations of up to ten unknowns, all of their answers where there are many, x = p + t v, and the nearest by least squares where there are none; its rows reduced, its rank and null space; P A = L U and A = Q R. From 6 columns on its boxes and its results are smaller, to fit the card, each number of a result to as many digits as fit, the whole of it shown as you point at it; the eigenvalues of a matrix larger than 4 × 4 are found by the QR algorithm, and where a matrix is badly conditioned, as the 10 × 10 Hilbert matrix is, the note says how few digits of its inverse, determinant or answers can be trusted. And of two vectors, 1, 2, 3, their dot and cross products, lengths, the angle between them and the projection of one on the other.
  • Complex works out a complex number, (2 + 3i)(1 – i), sqrt(-4) or 2∠30 in polar form, for its real and imaginary parts, length, angle and conjugate, and the n-th roots of one, each drawn on the plane; and the roots of a polynomial of complex coefficients, x^2 + (1 + i)x + 2i.
  • Base works out whole numbers as a computer keeps them, in 8, 16, 32 or 64 bits: decimal, 0xFF, 0b1010 or 0o17, with + – × ÷, mod, AND, OR, XOR, NOT, shifts and ROL and ROR, shown in decimal, hexadecimal, octal and binary. Bits shows each bit of a number, to turn over with a click, and how many are 1; IEEE 754 shows a number as a double and a float, 0.1 = 0x3FB999999999999A, what it is to its last digit, and the number of the bits of one. Any base writes a number of any base from 2 to 36 in another, the digits after its point as well, 0.1 as 0.0(0011) in base 2; Text and codes gives the code points, UTF-8 and UTF-16 of a text, é = U+00E9 = C3 A9, and a text of them.
  • Integer has the prime factors of a whole number of up to 20 digits, 360 = 2³ × 3² × 5, whether it is prime, and its divisors; the GCD and LCM of two numbers or more; a mod m, a^b mod m, the inverse of a and every x of a x ≡ b (mod m), and the Chinese remainder theorem, 2 mod 3, 3 mod 5; a calculation to every digit, 100!, 2^200, 50 nCr 25, fib(300); the primes before and after a number, π(n), the n-th prime and the primes from a to b; a fraction, a decimal or 0.1(6) as a fraction, a decimal with the part that comes again in brackets and a continued fraction; and a number rounded to d places, half away from 0, down, up and toward 0, and to d significant figures.

Using the Calculator

Click on numbers and functions in Calculator just as you would with a standard calculator.
The display shows the equation as you enter it, so you always see the operation to be carried out. Click the equation sign and the display shows the result, with the finished equation on the small line above it. The opened parenthesis button keeps a ring around it until all parentheses expressions are closed.

If an equation gets too long for the display, its font gets smaller first. After that, press the line, hold it still for a moment, then drag it left or right to see the rest; dots (…) mark the side where more is hidden. A drag without the hold selects a part of it. On a touch screen a drag slides it, and a hold selects.
If a calculation is not possible (for example 1 ÷ 0), the display shows Error and the line above shows the equation that caused it.

This calculator uses an algebraic method of entry that allows you to enter mathematical sequences in the same order that they are algebraically stated (PEMDAS / AOS™).
PEMDAS: "Parentheses, Exponents, Multiplication or Division, and Addition or Subtraction". Multiplication and division have the same ranking. Addition and subtraction as well.

Keyboard Support

There is full keyboard support for each button on the calculator.

If the pasted text contains an equation sign, it is calculated right away.
    Double-click a number in the equation to select it whole, or drag over a part of it: what you type or paste then goes in its place, and DEL takes it out.

Copy takes the part of the display you selected, or else the result. The first time you use Paste there, the browser asks you to allow the calculator to read what you copied; Ctrl+V works without that.   • All [ENTER] keys represent the '=' button.
  • Click Rad, Deg or Grad at the bottom left of the display to go on to the next unit, as the key does.
  • [←] and [→] move the cursor in the equation. [Home] and [End] go to its start and its end.
  • History: click the clock at the top left of the display to see the calculations you finished with '='. Point at an equation or a result to show its two buttons on the left: insert enters the equation again, so you can change it or finish it with '=', or puts the result in as a number in the equation you are entering; copy copies it. A calculation with sin, cos or tan is marked with the unit it was done in, Deg, Rad or Grad, and is worked out in that unit again. A calculation done in fraction mode keeps its fractions, and one done with decimals keeps its decimals; inserting it switches fraction mode to match.

[Del or Shift+Backspace] Click to clear the displayed number and the constant and pending operations.

About Precision

This calculator operates with a precision of about 15 significant digits (only). This is due to how JavaScript handles floating point numbers.
This isn't actually a JavaScript feature but a feature of the way the IEEE 754 standard defines how floating point numbers are stored in memory. JavaScript requires every number to be one of these 64-bit values, and any language that uses them, such as C, Java or Python, suffers from this 'feature' too.

Results are shown with 13 significant digits, so these tiny rounding errors stay out of sight: 0.1 + 0.2 shows 0.3, not 0.30000000000000004. Angles in degrees give exact values where they should: sin 180 is 0 and cos 60 is 0.5.

The problem is that JavaScript uses 64 bits of data, which can hold 18446744073709551616 different bit patterns, to store every number: 1 bit for the sign, 11 bits for the exponent and 52 bits for the digits. That covers values from about –1.7976931348623157e+308 to 1.7976931348623157e+308, with the smallest normal number at 2.2250738585072014e–308 and even smaller ones down to 5e–324.
A smaller precision value with some of the bits as an exponent is how it fits that range (this can also be done in 16 bits with a smaller range and less precision). The result is that it can approximate any value in the range but not exactly represent them all, because in real number terms there are an infinite number of values between any 2 given values.
As part of your calculation clearly the internal representation of the value is going outside the available precision and you are ending up with an approximation to the value instead of an exact value.

For Schools and Organizations

Administrators can disable specific calculator features that students are not permitted to use, including formulas, the unit converter, graphing, statistics, the solver, the numbers, Detached Mode, and the option to display the calculator on every page. These settings can be configured centrally for all managed students through the Google Admin Console or Chrome policies.

Once a feature is disabled, it is immediately removed from the calculator, and students cannot re-enable it. The setting is locked and displays the message “Turned off by your administrator.” How to set it up.
